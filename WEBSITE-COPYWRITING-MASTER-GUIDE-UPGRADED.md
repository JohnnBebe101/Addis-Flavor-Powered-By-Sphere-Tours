## 2. Voice and style system

### 2.1 Voice attributes

| Trait | Meaning | Copy behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Authoritative | Demonstrates knowledge without exaggeration | Use verified facts, clear routes, practical details |
| Warm | Makes visitors feel welcome and supported | Use direct second-person language and helpful reassurance |
| Locally grounded | Connects people to the real city | Refer to actual places, neighbourhoods, customs, and experiences only when verified |
| Clear | Works for international visitors and fast scanners | Short sentences, explicit actions, understandable terms |
| Premium | Feels thoughtful and considered | Avoid cheap urgency, excessive exclamation marks, discount-heavy language |
| Action-oriented | Helps a visitor move forward | Use direct CTAs: Explore Tours, Plan My Visit, Request a Quote |
| Respectful | Represents Ethiopia without stereotyping or exoticising | Explain culture with context; never reduce it to spectacle |

### 2.2 Tone keywords

```text
Informed
Welcoming
Specific
Calmly persuasive
Useful
Culturally respectful
Direct
Premium
Locally connected
```

### 2.3 Grammar rules

- Prefer active voice: `Our team will review your request.`
- Address the visitor as `you` and `your`.
- Use contractions where natural: `you’ll`, `we’re`, `don’t`.
- Keep most sentences below 25 words.
- Use clear, internationally understandable English.
- Use numerals for prices, times, distances, and durations: `4 hours`, `90 km`, `$75`.
- Use en dashes for ranges: `8–9 hours`, `1–2 days`.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons unless the locked component’s visual system requires another treatment.
- Use no more than one exclamation point in a local content block; usually use none.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives: `best`, `largest`, `most trusted`, `number one`, `world-class`.

### 2.4 Amharic and Ethiopian cultural terms

- Italicize an Amharic word on first use and explain it immediately in English.
- Use the English name first where comprehension demands it, followed by an Amharic term when relevant.
- Capitalize proper names of places, people, institutions, festivals, and landmarks.
- Use Amharic terms for education and context, not decoration.
- Maintain a controlled glossary; never use multiple spellings for the same term within the site.

Examples:

```text
*buna* (traditional Ethiopian coffee)
*injera* (fermented flatbread)
*jebena* (traditional clay coffee pot)
Timket (Ethiopian Epiphany)
Meskel (the Finding of the True Cross festival)
```

### 2.5 Say this, not that

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Experience the magic of Ethiopia | Explore Addis Ababa with local context, from museums and markets to historic churches and mountain views. |
| Our amazing guides will blow your mind | Request a guide based on your interests, language needs, and available time. |
| The best tour in Addis Ababa | Explore a tour with a clear itinerary, inclusions, duration, and booking information. |
| Book now before it is too late | Check availability for your preferred date. |
| Discover hidden gems | Explore places beyond the usual route, with local context and practical guidance. |
| We offer the lowest prices | Book directly with Sphere Tour & Travel for clear communication and direct support. |
| Luxury travel for everyone | Plan an Addis Ababa stay that fits your time, interests, and travel style. |
| Authentic local experience | Meet the city through its history, daily life, food, neighbourhoods, and people. |

### 2.6 Content quality standard

- Target readable international English, approximately Grade 7–8 where possible.
- Use headings, short paragraphs, lists, and tables to support scanning.
- Answer the main visitor question within the first paragraph of a page.
- Include dates, prices, and policy details only when supplied by an approved source of truth.
- Never write testimonials, customer names, review counts, credentials, awards, or operational facts that are not verified.

## 3. Claims and review governance

### 3.1 Approved factual posture

The platform may describe tours and locally provided support only to the extent verified by Sphere Tour & Travel.

The following require approved evidence before publication:

```text
Licensing and licence number
Insurance status
Direct-booking saving/discount claim
Payment methods
Tour prices and inclusions
Availability and group-size limits
Cancellation/refund terms
Guide qualifications or certifications
Vehicle standards
Hotel/guide/car partner claims
Response times and operating hours
Safety claims
```

### 3.2 Claims excluded from static copy

Do not use in static website copy:

```text
500+ five-star reviews
Travelers’ Choice 2026
```

### 3.3 Google Reviews

Google Reviews, displayed through the approved Elfsight widget, are the social-proof source.

Use this section copy:

**Heading:** `What Guests Share About Their Time With Us`  
**Supporting copy:** `Read feedback from travellers who have explored Ethiopia with Sphere Tour & Travel.`  
**CTA:** `Read Google Reviews`

Do not duplicate the widget’s current rating or review count in static copy unless a controlled, verified data source supplies it. Do not invent testimonials or selectively rewrite a review in a way that changes its meaning.

## 7. SEO and metadata rules

- Make each title unique and readable; aim to keep it concise in search results.
- Start titles with the user’s search intent where natural.
- Write meta descriptions as a clear page summary plus useful action—not keyword lists.
- One H1 per page.
- Use H2s to answer genuine visitor questions and organize the page.
- Use descriptive anchor text: `Explore day trips from Addis Ababa`, not `Read more`.
- Do not claim an exact price, rating, count, licence, award, or policy in metadata unless it is approved and current.

## 8. Content workflow and review controls

### 8.1 Before writing or publishing

- Confirm the page’s purpose and conversion action.
- Check the current product/service capability.
- Verify all factual claims with an approved source.
- Confirm that partner-service language is enquiry-led where inventory is absent.
- Check that local/cultural language is respectful and accurate.
- Confirm the destination route, CTA, and form outcome.
- Apply the metadata matrix or create an approved page-specific entry.

### 8.2 Before release

- Read copy aloud for clarity and unnatural marketing language.
- Check grammar, spelling, proper names, and consistent Amharic spelling.
- Verify all prices, dates, timings, and policy details.
- Check links and CTA labels.
- Confirm that Google Review widgets—not static claims—supply dynamic review information.
- Ensure no disallowed claim appears in headings, alt text, metadata, structured data, forms, or images.
- Check privacy text beside all personal-data collection forms.
- Confirm that all content is understandable on mobile in short blocks.

## 9. Copy quality checklist

A page is ready only if:

- It states the visitor benefit clearly in the first screen or first paragraph.
- Its service promise matches actual operational capability.
- It uses no unsupported superlative, review count, award, certification, price, or guarantee.
- Its CTA names a real next action.
- Its form/CTA destination matches the promise made in the copy.
- It uses the approved brand hierarchy where appropriate.
- It is easy for an international visitor to scan and understand.
- It uses cultural terms respectfully and explains them on first use.
- It contains a unique H1, title, and meta description.
- It contains no fabricated testimonial or static review count.
- It does not collect or expose personal data unnecessarily.
