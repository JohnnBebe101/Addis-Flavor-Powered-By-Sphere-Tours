## **1. BRAND VOICE & STYLE GUIDE**

### **1.1 Brand Personality & Tone Keywords**

**Primary Personality Traits:**

| Trait | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Authoritative** | We know Addis Ababa better than anyone. We speak with confidence born from lived experience. | "We've guided over 2,000 travelers through Merkato's 13,000 vendor stalls." |
| **Welcoming** | We make visitors feel like honored guests, not tourists. Warm, hospitable, genuinely helpful. | "Selam! Welcome to Addis Ababa. Let us show you the city we call home." |
| **Culturally Authentic** | We honor Ethiopian heritage without exoticizing it. We use Amharic terms correctly and explain them naturally. | "Join us for a traditional coffee ceremony—what we call *buna*—roasted fresh over charcoal." |
| **Persuasive (Not Pushy)** | We guide visitors toward booking by highlighting value, not by pressuring. We answer objections before they arise. | "Book direct and save 15% compared to online travel agencies. Plus, you'll get flexible pickup times and a dedicated local guide." |
| **Clear & Practical** | We respect travelers' time. We give specific details (prices, durations, distances) upfront. No vague promises. | "The Debre Libanos tour departs at 7:30 AM, covers 90 km (2 hours each way), and returns by 6:00 PM." |

**Tone Keywords:**
- **Warm** (not cold, corporate)
- **Confident** (not uncertain, hedging)
- **Specific** (not vague, generic)
- **Respectful** (not condescending, exoticizing)
- **Action-Oriented** (not passive, wishy-washy)
- **Locally Grounded** (not generic, could-be-anywhere)

### **1.2 Grammar & Formatting Rules**

**Amharic Cultural Terms:**

| Rule | Example | Rationale |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Italicize Amharic terms on first use, then provide English translation in parentheses** | "Join us for *buna* (traditional coffee ceremony)" | Helps international visitors learn cultural terms without confusion |
| **Capitalize proper nouns (names of places, festivals, historical figures)** | "Emperor Haile Selassie," "Timket Festival," "Mount Entoto" | Standard English grammar, shows respect for cultural significance |
| **Use Amharic greetings sparingly but authentically** | "Selam! Welcome to Addis Ababa." (once in hero or welcome section) | Adds cultural flavor without overdoing it or feeling performative |
| **Spell Amharic terms consistently** | Use *buna* (not *bouna*, *boona*), *jebena* (not *jebenna*), *mesob* (not *mesobu*) | Consistency builds trust; use standard academic/tourism spelling |
| **Provide context for cultural practices** | "The coffee ceremony has three rounds: *abol* (first), *tona* (second), and *bereka* (third, meaning 'blessing')" | Educates visitors, deepens appreciation, avoids superficial treatment |

**Numbers & Formatting:**

| Rule | Example | Rationale |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Use numerals for prices, distances, durations** | "$42," "90 km," "4 hours" | Easier to scan than written numbers |
| **Use commas for thousands** | "13,000 vendors," "2,400 meters" | Standard English formatting |
| **Use en-dash for ranges** | "8–9 hours," "$48–65" | Professional typography |
| **Spell out numbers under 10 in prose** | "We've guided eight travelers today." | Standard English style (but use numerals in specs: "8 hours") |
| **Use 24-hour time format for tour times** | "Departs at 07:30," "Returns at 18:00" | Clear, unambiguous for international visitors |

**Punctuation & Style:**

| Rule | Example | Rationale |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Use em-dashes (—) for parenthetical statements** | "Addis Ababa—Ethiopia's vibrant capital—is at 2,400 meters altitude." | More formal and readable than commas or parentheses |
| **Use colons to introduce lists** | "The tour includes: hotel pickup, expert guide, entrance fees, and lunch." | Clear, professional |
| **Avoid exclamation points (except in CTAs)** | "Book now!" (OK) vs. "We're so excited!" (Avoid) | Maintains professional tone; CTAs can be energetic |
| **Use active voice** | "Our guide will show you..." (not "You will be shown by our guide...") | More direct, engaging, easier to read |
| **Keep sentences under 25 words** | "The National Museum houses Lucy, the 3.2-million-year-old fossil. Your guide will explain her significance." | Improves readability, especially for non-native English speakers |

### **1.4 Reading Level & Accessibility**

**Target Reading Level:** 7th–8th grade (Flesch-Kincaid score 60–70)

**Why:**
- 80% of web visitors scan content (they don't read word-for-word)
- International visitors may not be native English speakers
- Clear, simple language converts better than complex prose
- Accessibility best practice (cognitive load reduction)

**How to Achieve:**
- Use short sentences (under 25 words)
- Use common words (avoid jargon, tourism buzzwords)
- Use active voice (not passive)
- Use bullet points for lists (easier to scan)
- Use subheadings every 2–3 paragraphs (breaks up text)

**Test Your Copy:**
- Run through Hemingway Editor (target: Grade 7 or below)
- Read aloud (if you stumble, simplify)
- Ask a non-native English speaker to review (if they understand, it's clear)

### **7.1 Amharic Terms Glossary**

| Amharic | English | Usage |
|---------|---------|-------|
| *Selam* | Hello / Peace | Greeting (use sparingly, authentically) |
| *Amesegenalehu* | Thank you | Closing (e.g., in success messages) |
| *Buna* | Coffee | "Traditional *buna* (coffee) ceremony" |
| *Jebena* | Clay coffee pot | "Coffee brewed in a *jebena* (traditional clay pot)" |
| *Mesob* | Woven basket table | "Meals served on a *mesob* (traditional woven basket table)" |
| *Injera* | Fermented flatbread | "*Injera* (fermented flatbread, staple of Ethiopian cuisine)" |
| *Doro wat* | Spicy chicken stew | "*Doro wat* (spicy chicken stew, national dish)" |
| *Timket* | Epiphany Festival | "*Timket* (Epiphany Festival, celebrated in January)" |
| *Meskel* | Finding of the True Cross | "*Meskel* Festival (Finding of the True Cross, September 27)" |

### **7.2 Tone of Voice Quick Reference**

**Do:**
- Use "you" and "your" (conversational, reader-focused)
- Share specific examples and stories
- Acknowledge concerns and answer them directly
- Use contractions ("you'll," "we're," "don't") for natural flow
- Keep sentences under 25 words
- Use active voice

**Don't:**
- Use overly formal language ("We are pleased to inform you...")
- Make vague claims ("amazing experience," "unforgettable journey")
- Sound like a brochure ("Discover the magic of Ethiopia!")
- Use passive voice ("Tours are led by guides...")
- Use exclamation points excessively (except in CTAs)
- Exoticize or stereotype Ethiopian culture
